Benchmarks

Labelled public datasets, kept separate from the BNY case. Only small development runs exist so far.

Comparability and limitations
  • All three methods share the corpus, retriever, model, assessor prompt and passage budget; Multi-Query gets the same number of queries as Graph-Guided.
  • Gold answers and verdicts are sealed and used only for scoring. Official test sets have no public gold, so dev/internal splits are used.
  • FinCausal is extractive QA with an added, clearly labelled distractor corpus; the current distractors are too easy (all methods find the gold passage).
  • AVeriTeC uses the released dev top-k sentence pool, not the full 11.5 GB knowledge store. Causal-claim subsets are very small.
  • n=12 per benchmark: no difference shown here is statistically meaningful. Full evaluation is pending approval of its cost.

How it works

The pipeline in plain English. Each component: what it is → what it does → why it matters, with a short example.

Workflow

Three ways to find evidence

Ordinary RAG

One broad search for the whole question, one judgment over all links.

Baseline.

Multi-Query RAG

A model rewrites the question into several searches; results are merged.

Same number of queries as Graph-Guided, no map.

Graph-Guided RAG

One targeted search per causal link; evidence is routed to that link and judged with its conditions.

Tests whether structure, not query count, matters.

Relevant vs. valid

Relevant

The passage is on topic and quoted word for word.

E5, June 2023 FOMC minutes: “Treasury yields and the expected future path for the federal funds rate shifted upward significantly. Meanwhile, broad equity prices also increased notably.” On topic, but co-movement only.

Causally valid

The text states the mechanism, before the cutoff, for the right link.

E4, June 2023 FOMC minutes: “with inflation still well above the Committee's 2 percent goal and the labor market remaining very tight, maintaining a restrictive stance for monetary policy would be appropriate”.

Genuine contradiction

The text negates the link, or is a counter-example with no other drivers named.

Illustrative wording, not from the corpus: “higher rates had little effect on valuations”.

Walkthrough: one real BNY link (E1)

  1. Input. July 2023 retail sales came in at +0.7% vs +0.4% expected.
  2. Graph edge. E1: stronger consumer spending → higher expected company earnings.
  3. Retrieve. The E1 question searches only documents public before 10:00 ET on 15 Aug 2023.
  4. Extract. A quote is found: “Consolidated revenues increased 14%” (McDonald’s, Q2 results).
  5. Assess. The original v1 run labelled E1 “supported”.
  6. Validate. The validator objects: this is past revenue, not a forward outlook, and nothing ties it to demand. Result: insufficient evidence.
  7. Path. Because E1 is not supported, the earnings pathway (E1 → E2) cannot be called supported.
  8. Evaluate. We record the change and its reason; no claim about stock prices is made.

Components in detail

Research objective
What
The question this project asks.
Does
Compares three ways of finding and judging evidence.
Why
Tells us if a cause-and-effect map is worth the extra effort.
Example
Does mapping “spending → earnings” find better evidence than one broad search?
Input: event, claim or question
What
The thing we start from.
Does
Sets what evidence is needed and, for history, the cutoff time.
Why
A clear input keeps every method on the same task.
Example
Event: retail sales +0.7%. Claim: “5G causes COVID-19.” Question: “Why did revenue rise?”
Causal graph, node, edge, path
What
A map of hypothesised cause and effect. Nodes are things (spending, inflation); edges are arrows between them; a path is a chain of arrows.
Does
Breaks a big question into small, checkable links.
Why
A chain is only as strong as its weakest link, so we check each link.
Example
Path P2: spending → inflation → rate expectations → equity valuations (E3, E4, E5).
Ordinary RAG
What
One broad search, then one judgment.
Does
Retrieves the top passages for the whole question.
Why
The simple baseline everything is compared with.
Example
“How did July retail sales affect the equity outlook?” → top 20 passages.
Multi-Query RAG
What
Several reworded searches, no map.
Does
A model writes N queries from the input; results are merged.
Why
Uses the same number of queries as the graph method, so any gap is not just “more searching”.
Example
Five queries such as “retail sales Treasury yields Fed outlook”.
Graph-Guided RAG
What
Search and judge link by link, using the map.
Does
One targeted search per edge, evidence routed to that edge, edge conditions and path position given to the assessor, then validation and path rules.
Why
Tests whether structure, not query count, changes results.
Example
E4 gets its own search: “inflation, federal funds rate, further firming”.
Evidence retrieval
What
Finding candidate passages.
Does
Keyword ranking (BM25) over an eligible corpus, same budget for every method.
Why
If the right passage is never found, no reasoning can fix it.
Example
Future documents (e.g., the July FOMC minutes) are removed before search.
Evidence extraction
What
Pulling exact quotes or answer spans.
Does
The model must copy text word for word; code checks it.
Why
Exact quotes make every judgment traceable to a source.
Example
A paraphrased quote fails the check and cannot support a label.
Evidence assessment
What
A model reading the quotes and giving a label.
Does
Labels each link supported, contradicted or insufficient; or gives a claim verdict.
Why
Same model and rules for all methods keeps the comparison fair.
Example
E4: “supported” because the Fed said restrictive policy is appropriate with inflation above goal.
Causal evidence validation
What
A rule-based second check after the model.
Does
Checks the quote is on the right link, published before the cutoff, states a cause (not just co-movement), is forward-looking when needed, and flags conflicts, unmet conditions and other drivers. It can only weaken a label.
Why
Catches known model mistakes. It is a safeguard, not proof of causality.
Example
E5 evidence about housing demand is “off-target” for equity valuations. Validator v2: “rates rose; meanwhile equities rose” is co-movement, so a “contradicted” label is downgraded to insufficient evidence.
Path-level reasoning
What
Combining link results into chain results.
Does
Supported only if every link is; broken only if a link has a validated causal contradiction (correlation alone cannot break a chain); contested if evidence conflicts; otherwise incomplete.
Why
One weak link must not create a “proven” chain. Opposing pathways stay side by side, never netted.
Example
E4 supported but E3 insufficient → P2 is “incomplete”.
FinCausal 2026 benchmark
What
Annual-report passages with causal questions and human-marked answers (UAM / Lancaster, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
Does
Tests whether the system extracts the right cause or effect span.
Why
Labelled data shows extraction quality objectively. It is not a retrieval benchmark, so we add clearly labelled distractor passages.
Example
“What reason is behind the COR being 5.3 percentage points lower than in 2016?” → “higher prior-year reserve releases following favourable development on liability classes and the non-repeat of the Ogden charge…” (training-set item).
AVeriTeC benchmark
What
Real fact-checked claims with four verdicts and annotated evidence (CC BY-NC 4.0).
Does
Tests evidence-based claim verification.
Why
Shows whether the pipeline reaches the right verdict, for causal and non-causal claims separately. It has no gold causal graphs.
Example
“5G causes COVID-19.” → Refuted.
Evaluation metrics
What
Scores computed only after predictions are made.
Does
Exact Match and token F1 (answer spans); accuracy, macro-F1 and confusion matrix (verdicts); gold-passage hit rate and MRR (retrieval); quote validity; repeat agreement; cost.
Why
Gold labels are hidden from retrieval and reasoning, so scores are honest.
Example
Token F1 0.67 = two thirds word overlap with the reference answer.
Oracle-evidence diagnostic
What
Giving the assessor the reference evidence directly.
Does
Separates “couldn’t find it” from “found it but reasoned wrongly”.
Why
Points to which stage to improve. It is a diagnostic, not a normal result.
Example
Oracle right but method wrong → likely a retrieval failure.
Limitations: what this MVP does not prove
What
The boundaries of the evidence.
Does
Prevents over-reading results.
Why
Labels describe what text says, not true causal effects. Small samples give wide uncertainty. The model may know later events from training. BNY is one event without gold labels. The validator’s word rules are crude and were written after seeing E1/E5 errors.
Example
A 12-case difference between methods is not evidence of superiority.
